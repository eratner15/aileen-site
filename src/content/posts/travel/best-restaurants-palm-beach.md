Palm Beach has always been a dining town, even when it pretended not to be. For decades, the food was secondary to the scene — somewhere to be seen between rounds of golf and charity galas. That era is over. The restaurants that matter here now take the food as seriously as the atmosphere, and the best of them manage both with real intention.

I've been eating my way through Palm Beach for years, and the list below reflects the places I actually return to. Not the spots that photograph well but disappoint on the plate. Not the new openings coasting on hype. These are the **best restaurants in Palm Beach for 2026** — the ones that earn the reservation.

If you're also planning time further south, my guide to the [best restaurants in Miami](/magazine/travel/best-restaurants-miami/) covers the other side of the coin.

## Café Boulud at The Brazilian Court

There's a reason Café Boulud has outlasted nearly every other fine-dining restaurant on the island. Daniel Boulud's Palm Beach outpost operates with the kind of quiet confidence that only comes from doing things correctly for a long time. The courtyard setting — tiled floors, palms overhead, candlelight reflecting off white tablecloths — feels effortlessly elegant without trying to impress you.

**Standout dishes:** The Dover sole is prepared tableside and remains one of the best fish preparations in South Florida. The seasonal tasting menu is worth considering if you're in the mood to let the kitchen lead. Their duck confit with cherry gastrique has been a fixture for good reason.

**Who it's for:** Occasion dinners, business dinners where you want the room to do some of the work, and anyone who appreciates classically rooted French cooking done at a very high level.

**Reservation tips:** Book two to three weeks out during season. Lunch is easier to land and, honestly, just as lovely — the courtyard in afternoon light is something.

## Buccan

Buccan changed Palm Beach dining when it opened, and it hasn't lost a step. Chef Clay Conley built something rare here: a restaurant that's both genuinely inventive and deeply satisfying. The space is energetic without being loud, and the small-plates format encourages you to order widely.

**Standout dishes:** The wood-roasted cauliflower with dates and tahini has become iconic for a reason. The short rib dumplings are rich and perfectly portioned. If the tuna poke tacos are on the menu, don't skip them.

**Who it's for:** Date nights, groups of friends who want to share everything, and anyone tired of stuffy dining rooms. This is the restaurant where Palm Beach loosens its collar.

**Reservation tips:** Weekends fill up fast — book on Resy as soon as your dates are confirmed. The bar area takes walk-ins and is one of the better solo dining seats on the island.

## Renato's

Renato's sits on a quiet courtyard just off Worth Avenue, and walking into it feels like stepping into someone's very beautiful private garden. The Italian menu doesn't chase trends. It doesn't need to. Handmade pastas, impeccable veal, and a wine list that rewards exploration.

**Standout dishes:** The pappardelle with wild boar ragu is the dish I think about between visits. Their branzino, roasted whole with lemon and herbs, is consistently excellent. For dessert, the tiramisu is traditional and exactly right.

**Who it's for:** Romantic dinners, multi-generational family meals, and anyone who believes the best Italian food is the kind that stays out of its own way.

**Reservation tips:** The courtyard tables are the ones you want — request one when booking on OpenTable. High season weekends require at least a week's notice.

## Flagler Steakhouse at The Breakers

The Breakers has several restaurants, and Flagler Steakhouse is the one I'd choose every time. It occupies a handsome room overlooking the golf course, and the menu is exactly what a great steakhouse should be: premium cuts, excellent sides, a strong martini, and no unnecessary complications.

**Standout dishes:** The bone-in ribeye is the star and deserves to be. The lobster bisque is rich without being heavy — a genuine accomplishment. Creamed spinach here is done properly, which sounds simple until you've had it done wrong everywhere else.

**Who it's for:** Anyone who wants a steakhouse that respects the form. It's polished without being corporate, and the service team knows the difference between attentive and hovering.

**Reservation tips:** OpenTable is your best bet. Request a window table for the golf course view at sunset. Jacket suggested but not required — though you'll feel right in one.

## The Breakers Italian Restaurant

Speaking of The Breakers — their Italian restaurant deserves its own entry. The room is dramatic in the best sense: vaulted ceilings, hand-painted murals, the kind of architecture that makes you sit up a little straighter. The menu draws from Northern and Southern Italian traditions with seasonal adjustments that keep regulars interested.

**Standout dishes:** The lobster fra diavolo is generous and sharply seasoned. Their fresh burrata with heirloom tomatoes is the simplest dish on the menu and one of the best. The osso buco is fall-apart tender and arrives with a saffron risotto that's worth ordering on its own.

**Who it's for:** Visitors staying at The Breakers who want the full experience, and anyone who appreciates dining in a genuinely beautiful room. It also works surprisingly well for families — the Italian menu is approachable without being dumbed down.

**Reservation tips:** Hotel guests get priority, so book early if you're dining from outside. Weekday evenings are significantly easier to get than Fridays or Saturdays.

## Imoto

Imoto is Buccan's next-door neighbor and sibling restaurant, and it brings the same creative energy to Asian-inspired small plates. The space is darker, moodier, and a touch more intimate. Think of it as the late-night counterpart to Buccan's broader appeal.

**Standout dishes:** The hamachi crudo with yuzu and jalapeño is clean and electric. Their ramen — offered in rotating styles — is unexpectedly one of the best bowls you'll find in the Palm Beach area. The miso-glazed black cod is silky and perfectly caramelized.

**Who it's for:** Late-night diners, anyone craving something beyond the Continental and Italian menus that dominate the island, and couples looking for a more intimate setting than Buccan.

**Reservation tips:** Imoto tends to be slightly easier to book than Buccan, especially for later time slots. Walk-ins at the bar are realistic on weeknights.

## Pistache French Bistro

Technically just across the bridge in West Palm Beach, but I'm including Pistache because it belongs on any serious Palm Beach dining list. The terrace overlooks the Intracoastal Waterway, the menu is bistro-classic with a few modern touches, and the whole experience feels like it was imported from the 6th arrondissement and reassembled in the Florida sun.

**Standout dishes:** The steak frites is the benchmark order — a good test of any French bistro, and Pistache passes it. The escargot in garlic-herb butter is indulgent and correct. Their tuna tartare with avocado and sesame is a lighter option that doesn't sacrifice flavor.

**Who it's for:** Francophiles, anyone who wants excellent food in a genuinely relaxed waterfront setting, and the person in your group who always orders steak frites. The terrace at golden hour is one of the better dining views in the area.

**Reservation tips:** The terrace fills first — specify your preference when booking. Sunday brunch here is popular and worth planning around.

## Sant Ambroeus

The New York import has settled into Palm Beach with characteristic ease. Sant Ambroeus brought its Milanese café sensibility to Royal Poinciana Plaza, and the result is a restaurant that functions beautifully at every hour. Morning espresso, midday salads, evening cocktails and pasta — it all works.

**Standout dishes:** The cotoletta alla Milanese is the signature and the reason to come — a perfect, golden, pounded veal cutlet that's hard to find done this well outside of Milan (or the Upper East Side). The cacio e pepe is restrained and precise. Their gelato is flown in from the same source that supplies the New York locations.

**Who it's for:** The crowd that splits time between New York and Palm Beach — they already know. But also anyone who appreciates Italian cooking that's refined without being fussy. It's an excellent lunch spot, particularly if you're shopping nearby.

**Reservation tips:** Lunch is first come, first served and moves quickly. Dinner reservations are recommended, especially on weekends. The outdoor seats along the plaza are prime real estate.

## Coyo Taco

Every dining guide needs an honest, affordable entry, and Coyo Taco earns its place. Originally from Miami, this taqueria brought its handmade tortillas and no-frills approach to Palm Beach, and it fills a genuine gap. The food is straightforward, the portions are generous, and you'll spend a fraction of what you would at the other restaurants on this list.

**Standout dishes:** The al pastor tacos — spit-roasted pork with pineapple and cilantro — are the move. Their guacamole is made to order and tastes like it. The birria tacos with consommé for dipping arrived on the menu recently and quickly became a regular order for me.

**Who it's for:** Casual weeknight dinners, post-beach meals when you're still in sandals, families with kids, and anyone who recognizes that great food doesn't require a tablecloth.

**Reservation tips:** No reservations needed. Walk in, order, eat well.

## Lola 41

Lola 41 splits its year between Nantucket and Palm Beach, and it brings a coastal New England sensibility to the island. The space is polished and preppy — navy and white, clean lines, the kind of place where you'd expect to see someone in a cable-knit sweater even in Florida. The menu leans into seafood with global accents.

**Standout dishes:** The truffle pizza is thin-crusted and dangerously good. Their sushi and crudo options are consistently fresh — the yellowtail jalapeño roll is a reliable order. The lobster roll, available in warm butter or cold with mayo, is a direct Nantucket import and one of the best in South Florida.

**Who it's for:** The Nantucket-to-Palm-Beach crowd, sushi lovers who also want the option of pasta and pizza, and anyone looking for a restaurant that's lively without being chaotic.

**Reservation tips:** High season is tight — book a week out minimum. The bar is a strong alternative if dinner reservations are gone.

## What to Know Before You Go

Palm Beach dining operates on a seasonal rhythm. **High season runs November through April**, and during those months, every restaurant on this list will be busier and harder to book. If you have flexibility, January and February are the peak of the peak — plan accordingly.

Dress codes matter here more than in most Florida towns. Palm Beach still leans toward polished. A few spots on Worth Avenue maintain a resort-elegant expectation, and even the more casual restaurants skew smarter than you'd expect. When in doubt, bring a blazer.

## FAQ

### What is the best restaurant in Palm Beach right now?

It depends on what you're after. For a classic fine-dining experience, Café Boulud remains the standard. For something more relaxed with exceptional seafood, Buccan continues to impress. And if you want the full waterfront evening, The Breakers Italian Restaurant delivers on both setting and substance.

### Do you need reservations at Palm Beach restaurants?

For most of the restaurants on this list, yes — especially during high season (November through April). Book at least two weeks ahead for weekend dinners at places like Café Boulud, Buccan, and Renato's. Weekday lunches are generally easier to secure on shorter notice.

### What should you wear to dinner in Palm Beach?

Palm Beach skews dressier than most Florida towns. Smart casual is the baseline — think a linen blazer or a good dress. Several restaurants on Worth Avenue still expect resort elegant. When in doubt, err on the side of polished.
