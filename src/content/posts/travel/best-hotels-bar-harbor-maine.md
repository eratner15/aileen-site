# Best Hotels in Bar Harbor, Maine: Where to Stay for Every Budget

I have a theory about Bar Harbor. The town itself is lovely — all salt air and lobster rolls and that particular shade of Atlantic blue you only get on the Maine coast. But where you sleep changes the entire trip. Stay at the wrong place and you spend half your time in the car. Stay at the right one and you walk out the door into something that feels like a movie about New England in autumn.

I have been coming to Bar Harbor for years, and finding the best hotels in Bar Harbor, Maine has become something of a personal project. Every trip I try somewhere new. Some places earn a return visit. Others do not. What follows is the honest list — the properties that are actually worth your money, whether you are here for Acadia National Park, a quiet weekend away, or a proper Maine vacation with the whole family.

If you are planning your first trip, start with our full [Bar Harbor, Maine travel guide](/magazine/travel/bar-harbor-maine-travel-guide/) for the bigger picture. This post is specifically about where to lay your head.

## Best Hotels in Bar Harbor, Maine for Waterfront Views

### Bar Harbor Inn & Spa

This is the one everyone knows, and for once, the reputation is deserved. The Bar Harbor Inn sits directly on the waterfront with views across Frenchman Bay that make you forget whatever emails you left behind. The property has three distinct sections: the main inn, the oceanfront lodge, and the Newport building.

**The room to book:** An oceanfront king in the lodge building, upper floor. You get a private balcony, direct water views, and the sound of the harbor at night. It is the kind of room that makes you cancel your dinner reservation because you do not want to leave.

**Price range:** $250–$550/night depending on season

**Worth knowing:** The spa is solid but not extraordinary. The real draw is the Reading Room restaurant for breakfast — sit on the terrace if weather allows. The location is unbeatable. You are steps from the town pier, the Shore Path, and every restaurant worth visiting on Main Street.

### Balance Rock Inn

If the Bar Harbor Inn is the classic choice, Balance Rock is the romantic one. This is a mansion-turned-inn on the water, and it has the kind of understated luxury that does not need to announce itself. The property is small — just a handful of rooms — which means it stays quiet even in peak season.

**The room to book:** The Ocean Suite. It is expensive and worth every dollar. A sitting room, a fireplace, and a soaking tub with views that belong on a postcard.

**Price range:** $400–$800/night

**Worth knowing:** Balance Rock does not allow children under 16 in most rooms. This is intentional, and frankly, part of the appeal for couples. The heated pool overlooking the ocean is the best kept secret on the island. Continental breakfast is included and better than it needs to be.

## Best Hotels in Bar Harbor, Maine for Boutique Character

### Bass Cottage Inn

Bass Cottage is the kind of place that makes you rethink what a bed and breakfast can be. This is not doilies and forced conversation over scrambled eggs. It is a beautifully restored 1885 cottage with ten rooms, each one designed with real thought. The owners care about this place in a way you can feel the moment you walk in.

**The room to book:** The Loft Suite on the third floor. High ceilings, a separate sitting area, and morning light that pours in like honey. It is worth the stairs.

**Price range:** $275–$475/night

**Worth knowing:** The multi-course breakfast here is genuinely one of the best meals you will have in Bar Harbor. I am not exaggerating. Expect things like lemon ricotta pancakes with blueberry compote using berries from a farm down the road. The inn is a short walk to the center of town but set back just enough to feel private.

### The Bayview Hotel

The Bayview is the newer option on this list, and it fills a gap Bar Harbor needed filled — a proper boutique hotel with modern design sensibilities. Clean lines, good lighting, rooms that feel current without trying too hard. It sits on the quieter end of town with water views from most rooms.

**The room to book:** A Bayview King with balcony. Simple, well-appointed, and the balcony faces the right direction for sunset.

**Price range:** $225–$450/night

**Worth knowing:** This is the best option if you want something that feels like a hotel rather than an inn. There is a difference, and some travelers prefer it. No shared common rooms, no chatting with other guests unless you want to. The on-site restaurant is above average for a hotel of this size.

## Best Mid-Range and Budget Hotels in Bar Harbor

Not every trip calls for a splurge. Some of my favorite Bar Harbor stays have been at places that cost half what the waterfront properties charge and deliver just as much satisfaction in their own way.

### Bar Harbor Grand Hotel

The Grand Hotel is the best value on this list. It is not grand in the Ritz-Carlton sense — it is grand in the "this is a lot of hotel for the price" sense. The building is relatively new, modeled after the historic Rodick House, and sits right on Main Street. You can walk to everything.

**The room to book:** A Main Street-facing room on an upper floor. The people-watching alone is worth it during summer evenings.

**Price range:** $150–$300/night

**Worth knowing:** Rooms are clean, comfortable, and larger than you would expect. There is no restaurant on-site, but you are on Main Street — you have a dozen options within two blocks. Free parking is included, which matters more than you think in Bar Harbor during peak season. For families, the two-bedroom suites are an excellent deal.

### Acadia Inn

The Acadia Inn is the practical choice, and I mean that as a compliment. It sits on Route 3 about a mile from the town center, which puts you closer to the park entrance than most downtown hotels. The rooms are motel-style — nothing fancy, everything clean and functional.

**The room to book:** A poolside room on the ground floor. The outdoor pool is surprisingly nice and a genuine relief after a day of hiking in Acadia.

**Price range:** $100–$200/night

**Worth knowing:** This is the hotel I recommend to friends who plan to spend most of their time in the park and just need a clean, affordable place to crash. The free shuttle into town runs regularly during summer. Continental breakfast is basic but included. At these prices in Bar Harbor, that matters.

## When to Book and What to Know

### The Best Time to Visit

Bar Harbor's season runs roughly from May through October. Here is how it breaks down:

- **June:** The sweet spot. Crowds are manageable. Lupines are blooming along every roadside. Prices are 20–30% lower than July and August.
- **July and August:** Peak everything. The weather is beautiful, the town is packed, and hotel prices reflect it. Book six months ahead or do not bother.
- **September and early October:** My personal favorite. The summer crowds thin. The foliage starts. Temperatures are perfect for hiking. Hotels begin to drop prices even though the experience arguably improves.
- **Late October through May:** Most hotels close entirely. A few stay open, but the town goes quiet. Worth considering only if you want true solitude.

### Booking Strategy

Book early. I cannot stress this enough. Bar Harbor is a small town with limited hotel inventory, and Acadia National Park draws over four million visitors a year. The best rooms at the best properties sell out months in advance.

A few specific tips:

- **For summer stays**, book by March at the latest. The waterfront rooms at Bar Harbor Inn and Balance Rock sell out first.
- **Cancellation policies** are generally flexible through the spring. Book early, adjust later if plans change.
- **Midweek stays** are almost always cheaper and less crowded than weekends, even in peak season. A Tuesday-to-Thursday trip in August is a different experience than a Friday-to-Sunday one.
- **Ask about packages.** Several of these hotels offer Acadia-themed packages that bundle park passes or guided tours. They are occasionally a genuine deal.

## Getting Around From Your Hotel

One thing worth mentioning: Bar Harbor is walkable if you stay downtown, but Acadia National Park is not. You will need a car or the free Island Explorer shuttle (which runs late June through Columbus Day) to get into the park.

If you are staying at downtown properties like Bar Harbor Inn, Balance Rock, Bass Cottage, the Bayview, or the Grand Hotel, you can walk to restaurants, shops, and the Shore Path with no car needed. The Acadia Inn on Route 3 is more car-dependent for town but closer to the park.

For a broader New England trip, Bar Harbor pairs well with a few days in Boston on either end. Our [Boston travel guide](/magazine/travel/boston-travel-guide/) covers where to stay and eat there. The drive from Boston to Bar Harbor is about five hours — long enough to feel like an adventure, short enough to do in a day.

Bar Harbor rewards the traveler who books thoughtfully. Every hotel on this list earns its place for a different reason — the waterfront drama of Balance Rock, the culinary joy of breakfast at Bass Cottage, the unbeatable location of the Grand Hotel, the practicality of the Acadia Inn.

My advice: decide what kind of trip you are taking first. A romantic long weekend? Balance Rock or Bass Cottage. A family trip built around Acadia? The Grand Hotel or Acadia Inn. A solo trip where you want to walk everywhere and eat your way through town? Bar Harbor Inn or the Bayview.

Then book it before someone else does.

The hotels here reflect that spirit. They are not flashy. They are just exactly right.
