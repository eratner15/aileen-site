# Where to Eat in Palm Beach: A Local's Favorites

Palm Beach has a dining scene that punches well above its size. For an island roughly fourteen miles long and half a mile wide, the concentration of genuinely good restaurants is remarkable — and it has only sharpened in recent years.

But here is the thing about eating in Palm Beach: the best spots are not always the most obvious ones. Tourist guides will point you toward the same three names. I want to tell you where to eat in Palm Beach based on where I actually sit down, order, and come back to — organized by the meal, because that is really how you plan a day here.

If you are putting together a longer trip, my [best restaurants in Palm Beach](/magazine/travel/best-restaurants-palm-beach/) guide covers additional picks, and for those splitting time between coasts, I have a similar rundown for [Miami dining](/magazine/travel/best-restaurants-miami/).

## Breakfast & Brunch

Morning is my favorite time to eat on the island. The light is soft, the tables are unhurried, and you can usually get in without a reservation.

### Sant Ambroeus

The Worth Avenue outpost of this Milanese institution does breakfast with quiet precision. The cappuccino is the best on the island — I am not being hyperbolic. Order the uova alla fiorentina (poached eggs over spinach with fontina) or keep it simple with a cornetto and fresh-squeezed orange juice. The sidewalk tables are ideal for people-watching, though interior seating has better air conditioning when the humidity climbs.

### The Honor Bar

Part of the Hillstone family, Honor Bar does a weekend brunch that I think is underrated relative to the hype around other spots. The lobster toast is excellent, and the burger — available at brunch — is one of the best on the island, period. The space is compact and dark-wooded, more club than café. Expect a wait on Sundays.

### Green's Pharmacy

I am including Green's because it is a Palm Beach institution and because the breakfast is genuinely solid for what it is — a lunch-counter experience in a pharmacy that has been here since 1938. Eggs, pancakes, coffee. Nothing fancy. That is the point. Locals have been coming here for decades, and there is something grounding about that when everything else on the island skews polished.

### Café Via Flora

A newer addition along Via Mizner, Café Via Flora strikes a balance between European café and Palm Beach brunch spot. The avocado toast is made with good bread and finished with chili flake and radish, and the açaí bowl is better than it has any right to be. Service is attentive without hovering.

## Lunch

Midday eating in Palm Beach tends to fall into two camps: quick and casual, or long and leisurely. I have picks for both.

### Buccan

Buccan is the restaurant that changed Palm Beach dining when it opened, and it remains one of the best meals on the island at any hour. At lunch, the wood-fired pizzas are what I order most. The mushroom pie with truffle and fontina is outstanding. The short rib tacos are another staple. The energy in the room is lively without being loud — a hard line to walk, and they walk it well.

### Imoto

Right next door to Buccan (same ownership), Imoto does Asian-inflected small plates that work beautifully as a shared lunch. The tuna poke is clean and bright, and the crispy rice with spicy salmon has a following for good reason. If you want something lighter than a full sit-down meal but more interesting than a salad, this is where I send people.

### Ta-boó

Open since 1941, Ta-boó is a Worth Avenue fixture with a menu that covers a lot of ground — burgers, salads, pasta, seafood. The cobb salad is generous and well-constructed. I would not call it the most exciting meal on the island, but it is reliable, the patio is pleasant, and it captures a certain old-guard Palm Beach feeling that you cannot manufacture.

### Pizza al Fresco

On the courtyard at Via Mizner, this is exactly what the name promises. Thin-crust pizza, eaten outdoors, surrounded by Mediterranean architecture. The margherita is the move. It is not trying to be anything other than a good pizza in a beautiful setting, and sometimes that is all you want from lunch.

## Dinner

Dinner is where Palm Beach tightens up. Reservations matter. A collared shirt is not a bad idea at most of these places (required at some). And the cooking, at its best, is genuinely impressive.

### Café Boulud

Daniel Boulud's restaurant at The Brazilian Court Hotel is the finest dinner on the island, and I do not think it is particularly close. The menu shifts seasonally, but the Dover sole meunière is a constant worth ordering every time. The pâté en croûte, when available, is textbook. The courtyard dining room — open air, lantern-lit, surrounded by palms — is one of the most beautiful restaurant settings in South Florida. Worth the reservation. Worth dressing up for.

### Renato's

Hidden in a courtyard off Via Mizner, Renato's is the kind of Italian restaurant that Palm Beach does exceptionally well: white tablecloths, serious wine list, pasta made with care. The osso buco is rich and deeply satisfying. The branzino, prepared simply with lemon and olive oil, is another strong choice. The courtyard seating on a warm evening feels private and slightly magical — like eating in someone's very elegant garden.

### Flagler Steakhouse

Located at The Breakers, Flagler Steakhouse sits overlooking the golf course with views that stretch to the Intracoastal. The bone-in ribeye is the centerpiece of the menu, and it delivers. Sides are classic steakhouse — creamed spinach, baked potato, wedge salad. The setting does a lot of work here, especially at sunset, but the kitchen holds up its end of the deal. Not the most inventive meal in Palm Beach, but not everything needs to be.

### Flamingo Grill

An old-school supper club that has been serving Palm Beach since the 1950s. The Caesar salad is prepared tableside. The rack of lamb is excellent. There is something about eating here that feels deliberately out of time — and I mean that as a compliment. If you appreciate restaurants that have not been redesigned by a branding agency, the Flamingo is your place.

## Drinks

Palm Beach is not a late-night town. But it has a few spots where a cocktail or a glass of wine feels exactly right.

### The Living Room at The Chesterfield

Dark, intimate, with a pianist most evenings. Order a martini or a Negroni — the bartenders here are classically trained and it shows. This is where I go when I want a drink that feels like an occasion without the scene of a louder bar.

### Cucina Palm Beach

The bar at Cucina is worth visiting even if you are not staying for dinner. The wine list skews Italian and is well-priced by Palm Beach standards. A glass of Barolo and a plate of burrata makes for a perfect pre-dinner stop or a light evening on its own.

### HMF at The Breakers

The Breakers' main bar is grand in scale — high ceilings, ocean energy, well-dressed crowds. The cocktail program is sharp, with seasonal menus that rotate thoughtfully. It can get busy on weekends during season, but a Tuesday or Wednesday evening here is one of my favorite low-key plans.

## A Few Practical Notes

**Seasonality matters.** Palm Beach dining season runs from November through April. During these months, restaurants are busier, reservations are harder to come by, and some menus adjust to reflect the influx of seasonal residents. Off-season (May through October), several spots close or reduce hours, but those that stay open are often more relaxed and easier to get into.

**Dress codes still exist here.** Not everywhere, but at places like Café Boulud, Flagler Steakhouse, and The Breakers properties, you will want to dress appropriately. Smart casual is the baseline. When in doubt, err on the side of slightly overdressed.

**Parking is limited on the island.** Valet is common at dinner spots. For Worth Avenue lunch and shopping, the public lots fill up on weekends during season, so plan accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best restaurant in Palm Beach for a special occasion?

Café Boulud at The Brazilian Court is the top choice for celebrations. The French-American menu is polished without being stuffy, and the courtyard setting feels appropriately festive. Book at least two weeks ahead during season.

### Do you need reservations to eat in Palm Beach?

During season (November through April), yes — especially for dinner. Restaurants like Café Boulud, Buccan, and Renato's fill up days in advance. For breakfast and lunch, walk-ins are usually fine on weekdays, but weekends can be a different story.

### Where should I eat in Palm Beach on a budget?

Palm Beach is not the most budget-friendly dining town, but lunch is always more approachable than dinner. Try the pizza at Buccan or the sandwiches at Sant Ambroeus for satisfying meals that won't run up a three-figure tab. Just over the bridge in West Palm, you'll find even more affordable options.
