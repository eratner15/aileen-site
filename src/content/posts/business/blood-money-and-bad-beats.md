## THE PENTHOUSE

**Manhattan, June 2023
**
The air hung thick with Cohiba smoke and 23-year Pappy Van Winkle, the kind of excess that signals either arrival or desperation, never both. At the Penthouse, 43 floors above Manhattan, it was always both.Tech billionaires, real estate moguls, and hedge fund sharks hunched over a custom poker table, watching lavender and pumpkin chips pile into a small mountain at the center.

Damon “Dee” Jones owned the room.

At fifty-three, fighting to look forty, Jones had his old NBA physique vacuum-sealed into an $8,000 Tom Ford suit. Expensive tailoring over fading glory. He was the “Face Card,” a term that would later appear in federal indictments as something approximating “bait.” The celebrity who made John Doe #1, a tech CEO worth ten figures, comfortable enough to slide $100,000 into the pot.

“Gentlemen,” Jones said, his smile still electric after all these years, “welcome to the real game.”

He was playing his role to perfection, telling stories about his old teammate, the one everyone called **The King**. The indictment identifies **The King** as **Player 3**, a prominent NBA figure who played for the Cavs and now the Lakers, and one Jones had an inside track on.

 The table leaned in, rapt. Then someone interrupted:
> 
> “That was what, fifteen years ago, Dee?”
> 
The sting landed harder than intended. Jones clocked the CEO’s Richard Mille—$300,000 wrist wear, the same model he’d seen on a rookie last week, a kid literally not born when Jones won his championship. For a microsecond, something broke in his face. Then he swallowed the burn, recalibrated, cranked his smile back to full wattage. The muscle memory was perfect. Everything else was improvisation.

Jones played his role so perfectly that no one noticed the other men at the table. The men who had no business being there. The men who would, in October 2025, be named in federal indictments as members and associates of the Bonanno, Gambino, and Genovese crime families.

## THE PLAYERS

The room was a carefully constructed ecosystem of deceit, a fusion of elite celebrity, advanced technology, and organized crime.

### The Face Cards (The Bait)

- 
**Damon “Dee” Jones**: The host , the celebrity magnet. As a member of the **Cheating Teams**, his job was to leverage his NBA past to lure in the “whales” and make them feel like they were in a safe, exclusive club.
- 
**Chauncey Billups**: The other former NBA champion and current coach listed in the indictment as a **Face Card**. Though not at this specific game, his presence at others, like the rigged games in Las Vegas , lent the entire operation a powerful air of legitimacy and cover. [Click here for a first person account of Chauncey's games](https://x.com/OnlyFriends_Pod/status/1981379130190156129)


### The Cheating Team (The House)

- 
**Ammar “Flapper Poker” Awawdeh**: A professional player and, as an associate of the **Gambino Crime Family**, a key organizer and on-site crew boss.
- 
**Zhen Hu, called “Scruli”**: The “Quarterback” or “Driver”. An MIT-trained mind who allegedly calculated odds , he was the player at the table receiving the secret signals from the remote operator and making the impossible “hero calls” that stole millions.
- 
**Shane “Sugar” Hennen**: A member of the Cheating Teams who, along with Earnest, surveyed the room like a pit boss. He was charged in both the poker and the betting schemes.


### The Mob (The Muscle)

- 
**Louis Apicella, known as “Lou Ap”**: A longtime associate of the **Gambino Crime Family**. He wasn’t there to play; he was a member of the Cheating Teams and was there to watch, ensuring the game ran smoothly and, more importantly, that all debts were honored, sometimes through threats of force and violence.


### The Masterminds (The Bar)

- 
**Eric “Spook” Earnest**: The operation’s key logistician. He stood apart counting the night’s take. Along with Hennen, he was the only individual charged in both the illegal betting and the rigged poker conspiracies, running the logistics, supplying the technology, and connecting all the pieces—from the mob to the celebrities.


The house ran like a Swiss watch. 

Awawdeh handled the mechanical, the cards, the chips, the flow. 

Hu handled the mathematics: the odds, the probabilities, the whispered angles that made “sure bets” possible. 

Apicella handled the enforcement, the ancient Gambino family tradition of ensuring that losing was never an option if you owed the wrong people money.

Earnest and Hennen were the connective tissue. They didn’t run games; they ran networks. The poker room was just one mouth of a two-headed creature. The indictment would call it a “conspiracy.” One head fed wealthy marks through card games. The other fed on information from inside the NBA itself.

The shuffling machine whirred, its mechanical sound the only honest noise in the room.

Or so the CEO believed.

## THE SHUFFLING MACHINE

What made this operation different from every mob poker game in the last fifty years was what sat in the corner of the Penthouse: a rigged shuffling machine.

Not a marked deck. Not a sophisticated sleight of hand. A machine. MIT-level engineering married to organized crime tradecraft. The shuffler didn’t randomize cards, it orchestrated them. Hu, with his MIT training, understood the mathematics. Awawdeh, the shuffle master, understood the mechanics. Together, they built a system where cards weren’t randomized. They were orchestrated.

In September 2023, when a rival crew attempted to use their own cheating technology, federal prosecutors allege Awawdeh and associates escalated to violence. A **Hobbs Act Robbery Conspiracy**. A gunpoint robbery. Not over money, over the machine itself. Because in this world, the technology wasn’t an advantage. It was everything.

## THE HAND

**All in.**

*One point eight million dollars.
Pocket aces.
A monster.*

Scruli’s fingers tapped his chips in a pattern: once, twice, pause, three times. A tell disguised as a nervous tic. A signal disguised as a tell.

Three blocks away, in a federal surveillance van that would later yield 147 hours of recorded video evidence according to court filings, an operator whispered into his headset: “River’s a king.”

Scruli called. Cards flipped.

The CEO’s aces should have won. *A statistical favorite in 99.9% of poker scenarios,*
Scruli’s three kings shouldn’t have existed. Not with two kings already dead, not with odds that make such a hand appear once in roughly five thousand shuffles.
> 
> John Doe #1 had just lost $1.8 million to mathematical impossibility or rather to engineering.
> 
Jones’s hand twitched toward the chips before he caught himself, the same nervous tic from his free-throw days. Quick mental math: his cut came to $90,000. Three months on the Houston house, he couldn’t sell. Half what he used to make in a single week.

Jones became the perfect host, clapping the devastated CEO’s shoulder. “Tough beat, brother. House is hot tonight.”

By dawn, the whale had stumbled home, lighter by nearly two million. Jones collected his cut from Earnest at the bar, $90,000, roughly three months of his Houston mortgage on a house he couldn’t sell. 

He watched Earnest count the night’s take with the precision of someone who’d been moving mob money for years.

This was easier than anything Jones had known in a decade. But it wasn’t real money. Not NBA money. Not the kind that built legacies. watching him count the night’s take.

This was the easiest money Jones had ever made.

But it wasn’t real money. Not NBA money.

**The real money, the money that would eventually appear in federal indictments as “*****proceeds from wire fraud conspiracy,*****” came from what he knew.**

## THE TEXT

**February 9, 2023**
**Houston, Texas**

Jones sat in his home office, surrounded by framed jerseys and his 2006 Three-Point Shootout trophy. His phone buzzed.
> 
> “Bron’s out tonight. Lower back. They’re keeping it quiet until 6:30.”
> 
LeBron James would sit out against the Bucks with a lower back injury. The betting lines had no idea. More importantly, the sportsbooks didn’t know. The algorithms didn’t know. 

The market had priced in a Bucks game against full-strength Los Angeles. In roughly four hours, that market would move dramatically, but only for people who’d received a text message before 6:30 p.m.

Jones walked past the jerseys of better days and typed frantically to Eric “Spook” Earnest, the same man who’d been counting poker money hours earlier:
> 
> “Get a big bet on Milwaukee tonight before the information is out! Player 3 is out tonight.”
> 
Then came the desperate addition:
> 
> **“Bet enough so Djones can eat to now!!!”**
> 
It was a typo federal prosecutors would later circle in red, a single, Freudian slip that captured the entire crime: the fall from grace, the desperation, the pure, unadulterated hunger.

This wasn’t friendship. It was an asset.
This wasn’t gossip. It was an invoice.

Earnest and Hennen activated their network with the precision of algo traders. Within minutes before the injury was public, while the market still thought LeBron was playing. Roughly $200,000 flowed through a coordinated cascade of betting apps: DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, and others. Multiple accounts. Multiple names. The indictment would later allege this was done to avoid triggering the sportsbooks’ algorithms designed to flag suspicious patterns.

**The score:**

- 
Milwaukee won by 18
- 
Jones made $15,000
- 
LeBron made headlines
- 
Earnest and Hennen made significantly more, the indictment alleges roughly $7.8 million stolen through the entire operation before it unraveled


Everyone got what they paid for. The government got a conspiracy.

The economics had inverted: Jones once earned millions setting up LeBron’s greatness. 

Now he made thousands, desperation money, mortgage money. Selling news of his absence. The former All-Star had become a news source. Worse: he’d become a liability. Everyone in the network knew what he knew. And everyone involved now had a federal problem if anyone talked.

## THE TWO-HEADED DRAGON

Eric “Spook” Earnest and Shane “Sugar” Hennen ran more than poker rooms. According to federal prosecutors, they operated a two-headed criminal enterprise sanctioned by three organized crime families: the Bonanno, Gambino, and Genovese clans, with each head feeding the same corrupt body

### Head One: The Poker Empire

This was not a backroom game; it was a high-tech heist, blessed by three organized crime families.

This is the world Jones and Billups, NBA champions, marketable names, social credentials chose as their clubhouse. They were the “Face Cards,” the celebrity alibi.

- 
The mob provided the muscle.
- 
The technology provided the victory.
- 
The celebrities provided plausible deniability.


### Head Two: The Betting Syndicate

The betting syndicate was built on a simple premise: NBA players have access to non-public information, injuries, lineup decisions, and strategic adjustments that the betting markets don’t have. Professional oddsmakers have thousands of data points, but they don’t have what an NBA player has: the voice of the coach in the locker room, the status of a teammate’s injury, the knowledge that tonight the team is tanking.

The syndicate built a supply chain. Players supplied information. Handlers like Long Phi Pham received it. Earnest and Hennen processed it. Betting apps distributed it. Money moved through coordinated accounts. By the time sportsbooks noticed the pattern, the bets were already placed.

**The Player: March 23, 2023**
Terry “Scary Terry” Rozier’s childhood friend Deniro “Niro” Laster was broke. Not “I have cash flow problems” broke. Genuinely broke. According to the indictment, Laster owed money to people connected to the operation. Then Rozier offered a solution: fake an injury in the game against New Orleans.

Rozier suited up. Played nine minutes. Left claiming a lower-body injury. The Hornets scored 24 points with him on the court. After he left, they scored 25 in the remaining three quarters. Bettors who’d shorted his scoring “under” won decisively. Laster’s cut: enough to solve his immediate problem.

A few days later, Laster drove the cash from Philadelphia to Rozier’s home in Charlotte. There, according to federal agents who would later surveil the house, the two childhood friends stayed up counting the cash together.

**The Coach: March 24, 2023**
The most shocking source came from the top. Federal prosecutors filed the betting scheme indictment and carefully did not name everyone involved. Instead, they labeled certain defendants as “Unindicted Co-Conspirators,” a legal designation meaning “we have evidence but aren’t charging them. Yet.”

*Co-Conspirator 8* was described as:

- 
Oregon resident
- 
NBA player from 1997 to 2014
- 
NBA coach since 2021


Three facts. One person.** Chauncey Billups.**

The 2013 NBA Finals MVP. The Pistons’ third championship piece. The man coaching the Portland Trail Blazers in 2023 was, according to federal prosecutors, simultaneously feeding inside information to a criminal network connected to three mob families.

Billups played two roles simultaneously: “Face Card” in the poker room and “Insider Source” for the betting syndicate.

The indictment alleges “Co-Conspirator 8” messaged “Spook” Earnest with information about his own team’s strategic decision: the Portland Trail Blazers would “tank” their game against the Chicago Bulls. Not just lose, but tank—deliberately field a weakened roster and accept a blowout loss.

The Blazers sat their stars.

Portland lost 128-100.

A 28-point deficit is not an accident. It’s a message. It’s a decision. And according to federal prosecutors, that decision came from the head coach and flowed directly into the betting network.

The “Face Cards” weren’t just bait for the poker rooms. They were sources, feeding inside information to the *same* criminal network.

## THE UNRAVELING 

By spring, the syndicate’s structure had become baroque in its complexity. Seven to ten NBA players are connected through handlers. Multiple betting apps used to distribute bets. A poker operation running in parallel. Mob families collecting percentages. Earnest and Hennen orchestrating it all.

And then everything broke because of someone nobody expected: a benchwarmer.

Jontay Porter was exactly the kind of nobody who breaks everything.

A benchwarmer on the Toronto Raptors, Porter made roughly $2.7 million annually, an NBA minimum salary that seemed like a fortune to most people but looked like scraps to someone like Long Phi Pham, who offered him significantly more to fake injuries.

**January 26, 2024**: Porter left the game after 4 minutes.

**March 20, 2024**: 3 minutes before claiming illness.

The pattern was statistically impossible. An NBA player doesn’t leave a game after 3-4 minutes with random injuries twice in eight weeks. The sportsbooks’ algorithms, machine learning systems trained to detect exactly this kind of fraud, flagged it immediately, beginning investigation protocols.

Porter had made approximately $2.4 million in betting payouts. He’d made himself visible. And in organized crime, visibility is a liability.

Federal agents pulled the thread, and it didn’t break:

- 
Porter (benchwarmer, $2.4M in payouts) → Long Phi Pham (handler, currently cooperating with authorities)
- 
Pham → Shane “Sugar” Hennen (poker operator, co-conspirator)
- 
Hennen → Eric “Spook” Earnest (poker operator, co-conspirator)
- 
Earnest → The entire network


The poker rooms connected to the betting rings connected to three mob families.

According to court filings, once the sportsbooks flagged Porter, the FBI escalated. Federal prosecutors began executing search warrants in October 2024. By October 2025, exactly one year after Porter first left a game early, the indictments dropped.

## THE END GAME

Damon Jones no longer played the Face Card. In U.S. v. Earnest et al., his name appeared on a federal indictment, not as a major player, not even as a co-conspirator, but as a defendant. A former NBA All-Star listed between “Lou Ap” Apicella and “Matty the Wrestler,” both identified in court filings as associates of the Gambino crime family

### The Dimensions of the Crime

**Defendants:** 33 individuals named across both indictments (U.S. v. Earnest et al. and U.S. v. Aiello et al.)

**Theft:** $7.8 million stolen through rigged poker operations; additional millions moved through coordinated betting accounts

**Organized Crime Families:** Bonanno, Gambino, Genovese (three major New York clans allegedly providing protection and infrastructure)

**NBA Players Charged:** Two (2) Rozier, Porter are facing wire fraud conspiracy and other felonies

**NBA Players Named as Unindicted Co-Conspirators:** At least 7 additional players (identifiable through indictment descriptions, including a head coach)

**Technology:** One (1) rigged shuffling machine, subject of a Hobbs Act robbery conspiracy charge

**Surveillance Hours:** 147+ hours of recorded video evidence from federal monitoring

**Timeline:** Operation active January 2023 - September 2024 approximately 21 months before arrests

### THE IMAGE

Jones stared straight ahead.
Not defiant.
Not defeated.
Just empty.

The smile that once lit up arenas had vanished. In its place: the flat, bureaucratic stare of a federal defendant. The Tom Ford suit was gone. The Penthouse was gone. The network that had promised him another shot at easy money was gone.

What remained was the indictment. Everything else was temporary.
